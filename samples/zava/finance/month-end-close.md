# Zava Month-End Close and Commitment Controls

Document: FIN-004, revision 2. Effective: 1 October 2026.
Owner: Finance Controller. Related documents: FIN-001 and FIN-003.

This is original fictional demonstration content, not accounting or financial advice.

## Close calendar

Business day 1: Accounts Payable checks received invoices against approved purchase orders and receipt evidence.

Business day 2: Cost-center owners confirm goods or services received but not invoiced. Finance records supported accrual proposals and separates open future commitments.

Business day 3: Finance Controller reviews accruals, reconciliations, and exceptions.

Business day 4: CFO signs the close package. An unsigned package remains provisional.

## Do not conflate these states

An open purchase commitment reserves budget; it is not automatically an expense or an accrual. An accrual requires evidence that the goods or services were received in the period and a supportable amount.

A vendor quote is neither an invoice nor receipt evidence. A purchase-order draft does not establish an approved commitment. A draft budget reallocation does not increase available budget.

## Required controls

Use separate requestor and approver identities. Match vendor, currency, purchase-order amount, received quantity or service period, and invoice total. Investigate differences rather than silently increase the purchase order.

A three-way-match exception needs the cost-center owner's explanation and Finance Controller approval. Potential duplicate invoices remain blocked until investigated.

## Demonstration case

If a USD 6,000 onboarding service has not started by quarter end, its purchase commitment alone does not justify a USD 6,000 expense accrual. If the service was completed and accepted before quarter end but no invoice arrived, Finance needs acceptance evidence and the agreed amount before proposing an accrual.
